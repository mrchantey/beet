# How not to eat too many beets

We are fans, not doctors. Beets are good food, and most people can eat them freely, but even beet lovers meet a few surprises. If something here sounds like you, ask a health professional rather than this website.

## The pink surprise

Eat enough beets and some people find their urine or stool turns pink or red a day later. It is called beeturia, it is the pigment betanin passing through, and on its own it is harmless. It is more common in people low on iron, so if it happens every time, that is worth mentioning at your next checkup.

## Oxalates

Beets, and their greens even more so, are high in oxalates. If you have ever had kidney stones, go easy, and eat beets alongside something with calcium, like the sour cream on [borscht](/recipes/borscht), which binds some of the oxalate before your body absorbs it.

## Sore bellies

Beets carry fermentable sugars that some guts dislike. If a big beet salad leaves you bloated, try smaller servings, or pickled beets, which some people find easier.

## A sensible tally

A serving is about one medium beet. One or two servings a day leaves plenty of room for the rest of the vegetable patch, and the [beet counter](/counter) will keep you honest.
